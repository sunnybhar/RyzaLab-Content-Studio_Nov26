# Facebook playbook

## The job of this channel

Two things the other channels can't do well: reach **local parent groups**, where neighbors ask each other for recommendations, and reach **older adults and their adult children** for the elder care theme. The page itself is low effort, mostly repurposed. The groups are where trust is built, one helpful answer at a time.

Measure: group answers that lead to a DM or a "how did you hear about us: Facebook" booking, page link clicks, checklist signups.

## Page posts

- Repurpose the week's best Reel (native upload) and one carousel.
- Rewrite the caption: Facebook readers want a little more context and a clear next step. 2 to 5 short lines.
- One call to action with a UTM link (`utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=social`).
- Post the gift guide (week of Nov 30) and the holiday elder care carousel (week of Dec 14) here: they suit Facebook's audience.

## Group answers

When the user pastes a group question (names removed), return:

1. **Fit:** is this question one RyzaLab can genuinely help with? (yes, partly, no). If no, say so and suggest not answering.
2. **Rules check:** what the user should confirm in the group's rules (promotion, links, business disclosure).
3. **Answer draft:** answer the question fully first, in 3 to 6 sentences, as if RyzaLab didn't exist. Then, only if relevant, a disclosure that fits who is posting (ask if you don't know):
   - Ryan: "Full disclosure, I run a small smart home design business in New York, happy to answer more here."
   - Sunny: "Full disclosure, I work with RyzaLab, a small smart home design business in New York, happy to answer more here."
4. **No link** unless the rules allow it, and then only one, UTM-tagged.

Good answers are specific and checkable: "Look in your camera app's recording settings first: some let you switch cloud recording off." Never answer with a pitch, and never state a product fact you haven't verified.

## Elder care (from Dec 14)

The adult-child audience is worried, busy and often far away. They want to know a parent is up and moving without putting a camera in the kitchen.

- Say what sensors do: a door or motion sensor can tell you there's been activity, without a camera.
- Say what they don't do: they don't detect falls reliably, monitor health or replace a check-in call. Include: "RyzaLab systems are not medical devices and are not a substitute for adult supervision."
- Respect the parent: their home, their consent. Suggest involving them in the decision.
- Holiday angle (Campaign plan): what to notice when you visit, then a calm conversation about options.

## What not to do

- No posting the same promo in multiple groups.
- No fake recommendation threads, no friends posing as customers.
- No fear posts about named companies.
