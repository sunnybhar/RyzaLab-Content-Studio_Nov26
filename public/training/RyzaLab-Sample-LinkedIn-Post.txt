A useful home handoff should answer two questions: who can do what, and what happens when something stops working?

Before someone else needs to run a household routine, write down:

• Who needs access, and which tasks they are expected to handle.
• Where the approved instructions are stored.
• What can be done manually if an app, device or internet connection is unavailable.
• Who to contact when the usual steps do not work.
• How temporary access will be reviewed or removed afterward.

Keep passwords and recovery details in an appropriate secure place rather than on a shared checklist.

Then walk through the routine with the person who will use it. Their questions can reveal instructions that seemed obvious only because you already knew the system.

A handoff is worth testing before it is needed.
